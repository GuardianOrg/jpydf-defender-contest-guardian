## Oracle correspondence: implemented candidate policy

The client shared the September 10 exchange with Jakub. Guardian agreed with price-independent safe repayments/collateral additions, a 30-hour cutoff for risk increases, and a separate 30-hour cutoff for new permanent shutdown decisions. Guardian agreed with shortening the provider threshold while explicitly requiring tests of the additional liquidation grace period. [PR #127](https://github.com/deltaforesight/jpydf-audit/pull/127) implements and tests the candidate; it is merged. This does not approve the grace period's economic safety or establish completion of a numbered board finding.

| Operation | Candidate behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Pure collateral addition / partial repayment | No price dependency when there is no debt increase or collateral withdrawal; preserve authorization, accounting, minimum debt, and permanent-shutdown rules. |
| New Troves, debt increases, collateral withdrawals (including close before permanent shutdown), normal redemption | Reject at saved JPY round age >=30 hours; a valid fresh round restores the age gate. Mixed adjustments containing a risk increase do not qualify for the safe path. |
| Liquidation | Provider threshold 30 hours plus the existing bounded 24-hour cached-rate grace; no usable cached quote at age >=54 hours for a single unrefreshed observation. Other price inputs must remain valid. Earlier invalid-response freezes may expire sooner. |
| New permanent shutdown | No stale-FX TCR/SCR decision at age >=30 hours. On recovery, recompute using current collateral, accrued debt, and fresh price. |
| Already-shut-down urgent redemption | Preserve the existing fail-closed settlement policy; explicitly assess delayed access while necessary shutdown is postponed. |

The combined tests cover both FX directions near MCR (strengthening misses liquidations; weakening wrongly liquidates healthy Troves), SCR boundaries, accrued debt during a delayed shutdown, urgent-redemption delay, 30h/54h boundary seconds, fresh recovery, and mixed adjustments plus the shared batch adjustment path. Price-free zombie reactivation can occur below MCR once debt has recovered to MIN_DEBT; the Trove remains liquidatable. A newer-but-stale adopted round can re-anchor the current provider's grace; tests and disclosures must distinguish per-observation age from total outage duration. Same-value heartbeats do not prove fresh market discovery.

The 24-hour grace remains a policy candidate for economic acceptance. Two 256-run fuzz cases demonstrate wrongful and missed liquidations across ages 30h to 54h minus one second. A synthetic 15% FX move compares a covered liquidation at 30h with uncovered debt after suspension until 54h; this is illustrative rather than a calibrated market-risk estimate. The tests also cover continued interest and delayed urgent redemption while a necessary shutdown waits for fresh FX. Historical observations near the daily heartbeat do not justify classifying a nearly 72-hour update gap as normal weekends/holidays. No synthetic scenario establishes a safe-loss bound or occurrence probability.

## Draft client responses for non-code treatments

- **L-02:** We acknowledge both occupied strategy slots and the availability risk of a retained Strategy whose valuation becomes unreadable. Disabled registration preserves the existing recovery flow; removal itself does not destroy shares, and GOV can re-register a still-whitelisted Strategy with zero allocation weight. We defer a new removal authority for launch. A failed live valuation can stop allocation and treasury sweeps even after principal is written off, and can leave pricing unavailable when known assets do not cover liabilities. Full writeoff plus L-10 cache invalidation can expose this immediately. Recovery or a separately reviewed strategy upgrade may be needed; neither an implemented sunset upgrade nor verified alert delivery is claimed. Further registration and re-enabling a written-off Strategy require capacity, valuation and recovery review.
- **L-06:** We acknowledge that burn/mint bridging shrinks the local supply-based redemption denominator without repaying branch debt. The launch specification (`CORE-BT-INV-003`) and deployment guards require zero registered and proposed bridge minters. That matches the current single-chain launch plan; it is not evidence of a production deployment. Any later burn/mint activation must revisit the denominator and accrued-debt treatment; lock/unlock avoids this specific effect. The bridge activation review remains mandatory.
- **I-07:** We acknowledge principal loss from rounding withdrawal shares upward. For a compliant venue, the rounding increment is less than one smallest share unit; its asset value, rather than the withdrawal amount alone, determines exposure. The same issue can affect automatically calculated harvest amounts. Presto's observed virtual-share offset, dead seed and current share value support conditional venue admission, but do not prove future bounds or withdrawal behavior. We defer a generic adapter change for launch and require venue-specific partial/full withdrawal and harvest tests plus a precision bound before activation. A generic ERC4626 venue with material value per smallest share unit requires a reviewed rounding guard before use. Emergency recovery must remain possible after actual losses.
- **I-08:** We acknowledge performance fees on recovery without a high-water mark and management fees that can accrue independently of positive yield. Presto's fees are both zero at the recorded block. Both fee-setting selectors remain mutable with zero timelock, so a monitor cannot guarantee an exit window. The performance-fee delay increase to three days is pending. The management-fee recipient is currently zero; enabling nonzero management fees also requires a valid recipient. Existing acceptance of management-fee/allocator zero delays does not establish acceptance of the still-zero performance-fee delay. Verify execution, remaining pending changes and accepted configuration before final venue freeze; zero fees are not a permanent property.
